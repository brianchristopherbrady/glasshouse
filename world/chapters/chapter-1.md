# Chapter 1

## 1.1.1

Noor is awake before the sun for no reason she'd defend, thumb moving on its own through a comment thread about the Halloway West cake — the kind of ordinary noise the Float produces by the hour, gossip with no weight to it. Except that it catches, the way a snag catches thread, and she opens Diagnostic Glass meaning to answer exactly one question: does Tomas Vale's Float-ID carry one filed correction, or more than one. She tells herself where she'll stop before she starts. She does not stop there.

The first sealed document — case AST-2291-RV-114, an authorization number she doesn't recognize, filed at a building that isn't the one the public record names — is not the kind of thing a person closes a tab on. It's a second correction on an identity that, as far as the ledgers she's allowed to see are concerned, only has one. She widens the query, cross-department, sealed case after sealed case, three more names inside twenty minutes, and somewhere in there a flag fires quietly to note that she's crossed a line she's crossed before. She knows what it means when she does this. She keeps going anyway.

She doesn't tell Mote. She doesn't tell B. What she has isn't a finding yet, just a shape, and she isn't ready to hand the shape to anyone before she's decided whether it's a pattern or her own hunger for one.

Mote doesn't need her to tell him. The query log gives him what it always gives him — not what she found, only that she looked, how far, how fast. One Float-ID becoming four sealed documents inside twenty minutes is not a subtle shape, and the Float-ID she started from happens to sit underneath a case that is currently his. He files the observation the way he files everything he isn't ready to act on: precisely, and mostly so that no one can later say he didn't notice.

Then he reads it back and decides. Tell B — but only the part the log actually supports, timing and scope, not a finding Mote doesn't have and isn't going to invent just to make the handoff feel complete. He considers, briefly, asking Noor first so he could arrive with a finished thought instead of an open one, and rejects it; that would be faster for B and worse for her, and he is not in the business of trading her privacy for someone else's convenience.

What reaches B, then, is deliberately incomplete: Noor pulled sealed records on Tomas Vale's Float-ID before dawn, unscheduled, and kept widening for twenty minutes. Scope and timing, nothing more, and Mote is careful to say so rather than let the gap read as an answer.

B reads it twice and notices two things, in order, and is honest with himself about which one he actually cares about. First: it's his case, and she went and stood inside it without telling him. Second: she was awake at four in the morning to do it, and that is not information about the case at all. He doesn't open a Dev channel. He doesn't file a joint-investigation request. He texts her — *what did you find, obviously, but mostly: you good?* — and tells himself, correctly, that the proper apparatus can wait long enough for the other question to get asked first.
